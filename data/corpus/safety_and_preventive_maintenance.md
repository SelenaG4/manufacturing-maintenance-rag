# Safety & Preventive Maintenance

## Lockout / tagout (LOTO)

Before any maintenance that exposes you to hazardous energy — electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic,
stored spring or gravity — the machine must be locked out and tagged out. The sequence is: notify
affected operators, shut down the machine normally, isolate every energy source (main disconnect, air
supply, hydraulic pump), apply your personal lock and tag to each isolation point, release or block
stored energy (bleed hydraulic and pneumatic pressure, lower or block suspended members), and verify
zero energy by attempting a normal start. Each worker applies their own lock; never remove another
person's lock.

## Stored hydraulic and pneumatic energy

Hydraulic and pneumatic systems hold energy even after the machine is switched off. Before opening
any hydraulic or pneumatic circuit, relieve stored pressure through the bleed valves and confirm the
gauge reads zero. Never work under a hydraulic ram or press slide held only by pressure — insert the
mechanical safety blocks. Cap open hydraulic lines during a repair to keep contamination out.

## Daily checks

At the start of every shift: check the way-lube reservoir, the coolant level and concentration, and
the hydraulic fluid level; run the spindle warm-up program; clear chips and swarf from the ways and
trays; and confirm guards and interlocks are in place and functioning. Log anything abnormal (noise,
leaks, alarms) rather than working around it.

## Preventive maintenance schedule

A basic preventive-maintenance cadence: daily — levels, warm-up, chip clearing, guard checks; weekly
— coolant concentration and filter, clean swarf separators, check for leaks; monthly — grease chucks
and check tailstock/live-center play, replace coolant filters, inspect way covers; quarterly — sample
and test hydraulic and coolant fluids, check belt tension and alignment; and condition-based —
replace bearings when the vibration trend crosses its alarm level rather than on a fixed calendar.

## When to escalate

Escalate to a service technician rather than continuing to run when: an axis reports repeated
following errors under light load (encoder/drive), a spindle overheats after coolant has been ruled
out (bearings), vibration keeps rising after imbalance and looseness are ruled out (bearings), or
hydraulic pressure cannot be restored after fluid, relief-valve, and leak checks (pump or internal
seal). Running through these to avoid downtime usually turns a planned repair into a larger unplanned
one.
