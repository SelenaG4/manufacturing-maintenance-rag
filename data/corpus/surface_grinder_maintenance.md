# Surface Grinder — Maintenance & Troubleshooting

## Wheel dressing and balance

A grinding wheel must be dressed when the finish deteriorates, the wheel loads up (glazes), or
burning appears on the workpiece. Dress with a single-point diamond in light passes. A wheel that is
out of balance causes chatter and a wavy finish and accelerates spindle-bearing wear; balance the
wheel after mounting and after every dress if the machine has a balancing system. Always run a new
wheel at operating speed behind the guard for one minute before grinding (a ring test plus a spin
test), because a cracked wheel can burst.

## Burning and poor finish

Burn marks (bluing) on the workpiece indicate too much heat at the wheel/work interface. Causes are a
glazed or loaded wheel, insufficient coolant, too heavy a down-feed, or too slow a traverse. Dress
the wheel, increase and aim the coolant at the grinding zone, and reduce the down-feed. A wavy or
chatter finish, as distinct from burning, points to wheel imbalance, spindle-bearing wear, or a loose
component rather than heat.

## Spindle vibration

Grinder spindles run at high speed and are sensitive to imbalance and bearing condition. Rising
vibration produces chatter marks and a poor finish long before any audible noise. Trend the spindle
vibration and act on the trend, not on a single reading — a steady climb in the overall velocity
level is the reliable early warning of bearing wear. Rebalance the wheel first (the cheap fix); if
vibration stays high with a balanced wheel, suspect the spindle bearings.

## Magnetic chuck

The magnetic chuck holds the workpiece; weak holding lets parts shift and can throw them. Keep the
chuck face clean and free of nicks (stone it lightly if needed), and verify the demagnetize cycle
fully releases parts. A chuck that holds weakly may have a failing rectifier or coil; do not grind
with marginal holding force. Never rely on the magnet alone for tall or narrow parts — use back-up
blocks.

## Coolant and swarf

Grinding swarf is abrasive and must be kept out of the ways and the coolant. Clean the swarf tray and
magnetic separator daily, and keep the coolant clean and well-filtered — dirty coolant recirculates
abrasive that scores the work and wears the ways. Check that coolant reaches the grinding zone; a
blocked nozzle is a common cause of burning.
