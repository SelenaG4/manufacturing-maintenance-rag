# Hydraulic Press — Maintenance & Troubleshooting

## Loss of pressing force / pressure

A hydraulic press that will not build full tonnage usually has one of: low hydraulic fluid, a worn
pump, an internally leaking main cylinder seal, a relief valve set too low or stuck open, or air in
the system. Check the fluid level and look for external leaks first, then verify the relief-valve
setting against the machine spec with a calibrated gauge. A pressure that builds slowly and then
holds points to internal leakage past the cylinder seal; a pressure that will not build at all points
to the pump or relief valve. Fault code HYD-07 is logged when measured pressure stays below the
commanded setpoint for more than three cycles.

## Hydraulic fluid and filtration

Use the specified anti-wear hydraulic oil (ISO VG 46) and keep it clean — over 70% of hydraulic
failures trace back to contaminated fluid. Check the fluid level daily, sample and test the fluid
quarterly, and change the return-line filter every 1000 operating hours or when the filter
differential-pressure indicator shows red. Cloudy or milky fluid indicates water contamination and
must be addressed before further operation. Keep the reservoir breather cap and its filter clean so
that dirt is not drawn in as the fluid level rises and falls.

## Overheating hydraulic oil

Hydraulic oil above 60 °C degrades quickly and thins out, worsening internal leakage and force loss.
Causes of overheating include a low fluid level, a clogged cooler, a stuck-open relief valve
(dumping full flow across the valve as heat), or continuous operation above the duty rating. Check
the cooler and its coolant/air flow, confirm the fluid level, and verify the relief valve is not
weeping. Sustained high temperature accelerates seal aging and is a leading cause of the internal
leaks that later show up as lost tonnage.

## Slow or erratic ram motion

Erratic, jerky, or slow ram movement is typically air in the hydraulic system or a failing pump.
Bleed the cylinder per the machine procedure, check the pump-suction line for leaks that could draw
air, and inspect the fluid for foaming. A ram that drifts down under load with the valve centered
indicates a leaking directional valve or cylinder seal and is also a safety concern — never work
under a ram held only by hydraulic pressure; use the mechanical safety blocks.

## Seal replacement

Main-cylinder seals are wear items. Signs that a seal needs replacement include external weeping at
the rod, a slow loss of holding pressure, and force that falls off over a production run as the oil
warms. Always relieve all stored pressure and apply lockout/tagout before opening a hydraulic
circuit, and cap open lines to keep contamination out during the repair.
