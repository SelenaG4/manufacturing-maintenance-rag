# CNC Lathe — Maintenance & Troubleshooting

## Chuck and workholding

Hydraulic and pneumatic chucks must be greased through their grease fittings every 40 operating
hours; an ungreased chuck loses gripping force and can throw a part. Check the draw-bar pressure
against the machine spec before each production run. Symptoms of a workholding problem include parts
slipping in the cut, chatter marks, and inconsistent diameters. Never exceed the maximum chuck RPM
stamped on the chuck body — centrifugal force reduces grip as speed rises.

## Tailstock and centers

The tailstock quill and the live center bearings should be checked for play monthly. A worn live
center bearing produces a squeal and a poor finish near the tailstock end of the part, and in the
worst case scores the center hole. Replace the live center at the first sign of runout above 0.01 mm.
Lubricate the quill with way oil and keep the taper bore clean and free of chips before mounting a
center.

## Spindle bearing wear

Spindle bearing wear on a lathe shows up as increased vibration, a rising spindle temperature, and a
degrading surface finish — often a periodic pattern in the finish that scales with spindle speed. A
rumbling or growling noise from the headstock at speed is a classic late-stage symptom. Confirm with
a vibration reading at the headstock (see the vibration guide): a steadily rising trend in the
overall velocity level is the reliable early indicator. Do not wait for the noise; replace bearings
on a condition-based schedule once the vibration trend crosses the alarm threshold.

## Turret indexing faults

A turret that fails to index or locks between stations typically reports fault code TUR-02. Causes
include low hydraulic pressure, chips in the coupling teeth, or a clamp/unclamp solenoid fault. Clean
the turret face, verify hydraulic pressure, and cycle the turret manually in setup mode. A turret
that indexes but does not clamp firmly will produce tool-position errors and must not be run in
production.

## Poor surface finish

Poor surface finish on a lathe has several common causes: a worn or chipped insert, incorrect
feed/speed for the material, insufficient or misdirected coolant, excessive tool overhang, or
spindle bearing wear. Work through them cheapest-first: change the insert, verify the feed and
speed, and check coolant delivery before suspecting the spindle. A finish problem that is periodic
and speed-dependent, rather than random, points at the spindle bearings.
